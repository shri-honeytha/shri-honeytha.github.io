Where it started

I’m happiest when I’m building something I haven’t built before.

Most of my projects begin with a simple question: “Can I actually make this work?” From there, I usually end up learning something new, breaking a few things, fixing them, and eventually watching an idea take shape on a screen. That process, more than anything, is what made me fall in love with development.

My journey has taken me through software development, AI/ML, full-stack applications, and agentic systems, but the thread connecting all of them is the same curiosity. I like understanding how things work, questioning why they work that way, and then trying to create something of my own.

Hackathons have become one of my favourite places to do that. I enjoy the chaos of a new problem, a blank codebase, a team full of ideas, and a limited amount of time to turn those ideas into something real. There’s something incredibly satisfying about starting with almost nothing and ending with something you can put in front of someone and say, “We built this.”

I’m still learning, and I hope I always will be. To me, being a developer isn’t about knowing every technology. It’s about being curious enough to learn the next one, stubborn enough to solve the problem in front of you, and creative enough to imagine what could be built next.
